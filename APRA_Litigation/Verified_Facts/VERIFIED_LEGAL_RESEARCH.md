## 1. MANDATORY REPORTING VIOLATION

### Statute: Indiana Code § 31-33-5-1
**STATUS: VERIFIED**

- Indiana is a UNIVERSAL mandatory reporting state
- ANY individual who has reason to believe a child is a victim of abuse SHALL make a report
- Standard: "reason to believe" - evidence that would cause individuals of similar background/training to believe abuse occurred
- Timeline: Report must be made IMMEDIATELY
- Report to: Indiana DCS or local law enforcement
- IC § 31-33-5-2: Staff of institutions (including police) must report DIRECTLY to DCS or law enforcement
- IC § 31-33-5-5: Employers CANNOT implement policies restricting/delaying reporting duty
- Penalty for failure: Class B misdemeanor (IC § 31-33-22-1)

### Application to This Case:
- Detective Robert Brazil: Law enforcement officer = mandatory reporter
- Victim Advocate Rochelle Ellis: Staff of public institution = mandatory reporter
- March 16, 2018: Both received disclosure of sexual abuse by cousin "Jaden"
- Both explicitly promised to CONCEAL the disclosure
- Quote (transcript 10:51-11:07): "I will make sure that it's between me and you and Rochelle. And that's it"
- No report was ever made to DCS
- No investigation of Jaden was initiated
- This constitutes a Class B misdemeanor for EACH mandatory reporter

## 2. KASTIGAR IMMUNITY PROTECTIONS

### Case: Kastigar v. United States, 406 U.S. 441 (1972)
**STATUS: VERIFIED**

- "Use and derivative use" immunity is coextensive with Fifth Amendment privilege
- "Use" = prosecution cannot use the immunized statements themselves
- "Derivative use" = prosecution cannot use ANY evidence gained as a RESULT of immunized statements
- Burden on prosecution: Must prove AFFIRMATIVELY that evidence is from a legitimate source WHOLLY INDEPENDENT of compelled testimony
- This is the constitutional minimum required to compel testimony
- Transactional immunity (broader) is NOT required but provides greater protection

### Application to This Case:
- David's immunity agreement provides use and derivative use protection
- Court reportedly ordered prosecutor NOT to use any information gathered
- If prosecution used immunized testimony or any evidence derived from it, this is a Kastigar violation
- Prosecution bears burden of proving independent source for ALL evidence used

## 3. JURISDICTIONAL VOID - PROBATION TRANSFER

### Applicable Law: IC 35-38-2-1(e) and Indiana Criminal Rule 22
**STATUS: VERIFIED**

Key Rules:
- IC 35-38-2-1(e): Probation supervision transfer requires CONCURRENCE OF BOTH COURTS
- Criminal Rule 22(C): Transfer of SANCTIONING AUTHORITY (including revocation power) requires CONSENT of receiving judge
- Receiving court assigns miscellaneous criminal case number when transfer ACCEPTED
- Receiving court must NOTIFY sentencing court of acceptance

### Relevant Case Law:
- Preston v. State, 591 N.E.2d 597 (Ind. App. 1992): Court without jurisdiction cannot revoke probation; revocation reversed
- Johnson v. State (Ind. Ct. App. 2011): Transfer requirements protect due process rights per Gagnon v. Scarpelli (1973)
- White v. State (1990), 560 N.E.2d 45: Once probation expires, court has no jurisdiction

### Application to This Case:
- Plea Agreement (Nov 2021) stated probation transferred to Porter County
- Porter County docket/CCS reportedly shows transfer was NEVER ACCEPTED
- If Porter County never issued an acceptance order or assigned a case number:
  - No concurrence of both courts as required by IC 35-38-2-1(e)
  - No consent of receiving judge as required by Rule 22(C)
  - Porter County had no jurisdiction to supervise or revoke
  - Revocation based on Porter County conditions may be VOID
- Post-conviction relief available under Indiana PCR Rule 1 if "court was without jurisdiction to impose sentence" or "probation unlawfully revoked"

## 4. BRADY VIOLATIONS

### Case: Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83 (1963)
**STATUS: VERIFIED**

- Prosecution MUST disclose material exculpatory evidence to defense
- Includes evidence known to POLICE (not just prosecutor)
- Prosecutor has duty to establish regular procedures for police disclosure
- Three elements: (1) evidence favorable to defense, (2) suppressed by prosecution, (3) material (reasonable probability of different outcome)
- Per United States v. Bagley: Disclosure required even WITHOUT defense request

### Brady Violations in This Case:
1. **Withheld video interview** (March 16, 2018): Contains exculpatory perpetrator-confusion inquiry by Detective Brazil AND disclosure of prior abuse by Jaden - both favorable to defense
2. **Perpetrator substitution inquiry**: Detective Brazil asked if events were "amassed by what happened with Jayden" - exculpatory line of inquiry never disclosed
3. **Concealed Jaden abuse**: Evidence of alternative perpetrator never disclosed to defense

## 5. SELECTIVE PROSECUTION / EQUAL PROTECTION

### Case: Yick Wo v. Hopkins, 118 U.S. 356 (1886)
**STATUS: VERIFIED**

- Race-neutral law administered in prejudicial manner violates 14th Amendment Equal Protection
- Discriminatory ENFORCEMENT is unconstitutional even if law itself is neutral
- "No reason for it exists except hostility to the race and nationality"
- Per United States v. Armstrong, 517 U.S. 456 (1996): Selective prosecution exists where enforcement is "directed so exclusively against a particular class of persons... with a mind so unequal and oppressive"

### Application to This Case:
- Jaden (cousin, likely white): Disclosed abuse concealed, no investigation, no prosecution
- David Jackson III (Latino/Puerto Rican): Full investigation, full prosecution, conviction
- Detective Brazil himself considered perpetrator confusion but never disclosed
- Hobart PD pattern: NAACP investigation (Oct 2022), officer racial remarks investigation (June 2020)

## 6. 42 U.S.C. § 1983 - CIVIL RIGHTS

### Statute: 42 U.S.C. § 1983
**STATUS: VERIFIED**

- Provides civil remedy for persons whose federally protected rights are violated by state actors
- Police officers may be liable for failure to disclose exculpatory material (Brady violations)
- Concealment tolling: Statute of limitations may be tolled when defendant actively concealed wrongdoing
- Municipal liability (Monell): Must prove violation resulted from official policy, practice, or custom
- Both Due Process (14th Amendment) and Equal Protection claims available

### Application to This Case:
- Detective Brazil and Rochelle Ellis acted under color of law
- Concealed exculpatory evidence (Jaden disclosure, perpetrator confusion inquiry)
- Failed mandatory reporting duty
- Pattern evidence of racial discrimination at Hobart PD
- Concealment tolling may apply given evidence was hidden
